## Why there is no number

`dc_model.py` fits one league at a time, and the repo holds 19 leagues. In England that is
the Premier League (`E0`) and the Championship (`E1`) - that is all. Where these four clubs
actually play in 2026-27, checked against the open-matchday result files for this season:

| club | division 2026-27 | tier | in this repo? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bristol Rovers | League Two (`ENG_4_league-two`, 14 matches) | 4 | no |
| Cambridge Utd | League One (`ENG_3_league-one`, 14 matches) | 3 | no |
| Leicester | League One (`ENG_3_league-one`, 13 matches) | 3 | no (its `E1` history stops 2026-05-02, before relegation) |
| Oxford City | not in the National League file | 6 or below | no |

So both ties are **cross-division by construction** - a cup tie always is - which is the same
problem as a European board: two scales that no single-league fit can compare, and no
inter-division matches in `out/matches.csv` from which to learn the conversion.

The repo now has a route for exactly this (`cross_league.py` + `elo.py`: ClubElo supplies the
conversion, the leagues supply the goal level, and a gate refuses when either side is
unmodelled or the ratings are stale). It is not available in this environment: the ClubElo
snapshot lives under `raw/`, which is generated and was wiped with the sandbox, and
`api.clubelo.com` is not reachable from here. On a machine that can reach it:

```
python tools/fetch_public_data.py --elo                     # one curl, no key
python predict_cross.py --home "Bristol Rovers" --away "Cambridge Utd" --odds 3.03 3.64 2.15 --date 2026-11-06
```

That would still return `PROVISIONAL` at best: neither League One nor League Two is modelled,
so the goal level would fall back to the pooled average, and only the supremacy would come
from a real source.

## Reading the market instead

This is the honest number today, and it is a good one: a de-vigged closing price is the best
single forecast of a football match that is publicly available, and it already knows the team
news that a goals model never sees.

- **Bristol Rovers v Cambridge Utd** — 30.8 / 25.7 / 43.5. A division apart (4th tier at home,
  3rd tier away), and the market makes the higher-ranked side a clear favourite without making
  it a formality: League Two clubs at home beat League One clubs often enough.
- **Oxford City v Leicester** — 12.1 / 18.2 / 69.8 on a 6.9% margin. Leicester are two
  divisions up but were relegated twice in two seasons, which is the sort of thing the price is
  already discounting; note the draw is only 18.2% because one side is expected to win clearly.

Two things the margin tells you: it is 7.0% here against 3.7-4.3% on the league boards, because
the book prices the extra uncertainty of a cup tie. Any edge you think you have has to clear
that surcharge first.

## What it would take to model these

1. `E2` (League One) history exists in the mirror and would take one line in
   `MIRROR_MAIN`; `E3` (League Two) exists only as the running season in open-matchday, so a
   League Two fit would rest on one partial season with no priors behind it.
2. The cross-division conversion still needs the Elo prior - adding the divisions does not
   make two leagues comparable on its own.
3. Even then, a cup tie is not a league match: rotation, motivation and a lower-division side
   raising its game are invisible to a goals-only model. The repo has no cup model and this
   report does not pretend otherwise.
